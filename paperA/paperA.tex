\title{Which Pair Is It About?\\
Query-Conditioned Verification in a Deployed\\Biotic-Interaction Extraction Pipeline}

\author{Anonymous ACL submission}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Literature-mining pipelines that populate biotic-interaction databases propose candidate
(taxon, relation, taxon) triples with a supporting passage, then filter them with a
sentence-level interaction classifier before curation. We audit that filter on 100 candidates
from a deployed system, graded by a domain expert, 65 of which are actually supported by their
passage. The filter is close to inert by construction: a rule layer runs first, so every one of
the $175{,}588$ candidates in the retrieval pool already carries two taxon surface forms and an
interaction surface form. It accepts 91 of 100 and reaches precision $0.714$, not significantly
above the $0.650$ obtained by accepting everything (one-sided exact binomial $p = 0.119$). We decompose its 26 false positives by
mechanism: 11 pair-binding failures, in which every component is correct but the relation is not
asserted between \emph{this} pair; 8 predicate misbindings; and 7 entity failures. Conditioning the
same decision on the candidate triple repairs 22 of the 26: a distilled 110M cross-encoder reading
the triple as a query reaches precision $0.934$ and F1 $0.905$ (McNemar $p = 0.018$), and recovers
6 of the 9 unleaked true positives among 50 curated pipeline rejections. We state the limit
plainly: at the deployed filter's own acceptance level the verifier reproduces its confusion
matrix, so the contribution is a tunable calibrated score where a fixed verdict stood. Entity
disambiguation and argument order survive the reformulation; we make no direction claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Biotic-interaction databases such as GloBI \citep{poelen2014global} are populated in part by text
mining. A retrieval system proposes candidate triples $(s_1, r, s_2)$ together with the passage
that allegedly supports them, and a filter decides which candidates are worth a curator's
attention. That filter is almost always a \emph{sentence-level} interaction classifier: it is
shown the passage and asked whether it describes a biotic interaction.

On 100 expert-graded candidates from a deployed system, that filter accepts 91. Its precision,
$0.714$, is higher than the $0.650$ obtained by accepting every candidate, but not significantly
so (one-sided exact binomial $p = 0.119$); its recall on supported triples is $1.000$. This is not
a tuning accident, and it is not carelessness by the people who built it. A rule layer runs
upstream of the classifier, and every candidate it emits already contains two taxon surface forms
and an interaction surface form --- on all $175{,}588$ rows of the retrieval pool. The property
the classifier was trained to detect is therefore, as a lexical matter, constant across its entire
deployment distribution. It was trained where negatives lack interaction language; at deployment
no negative lacks it.

What the pipeline actually needs to know is narrower: not whether the passage describes an
interaction, but whether it asserts \emph{this} relation between \emph{these two} taxa. We
decompose the filter's 26 false positives along that distinction and find three mechanisms
(\S\ref{sec:decomp}): 11 pair-binding failures, where every component is individually correct and
only the binding is wrong; 8 predicate misbindings, where the interaction term's real arguments
are other tokens; and 7 entity failures. The first class is invisible to any checker that
validates $s_1$, $r$ and $s_2$ separately, which bounds every component-wise architecture at
precision $0.802$ at full recall.

Conditioning the decision on the candidate triple repairs 22 of the 26. A 110M cross-encoder,
distilled from a teacher that was itself asked the triple-specific question, reaches precision
$0.934$ at recall $0.877$ (\S\ref{sec:results}), clearing the component-wise bound by $0.154$ at
its own recall, and recovers true positives the pipeline had already discarded. Removing the query
from its input at inference collapses its ranking to near the base rate, which is the controlled
evidence that the reformulation rather than the encoder is doing the work.

We also state the ceiling, because a reviewer will find it otherwise. At 91 accepts --- the
deployed filter's own operating point --- the verifier reproduces its confusion matrix.
With 65 supported triples among 100, any system accepting 91 incurs at least 26 false positives,
and both systems attain that floor. The contribution is therefore not superiority at the deployed
operating point, which is unavailable to anyone, but the existence of an operating curve where a
fixed verdict stood: a curator can take 84 candidates at precision $0.774$ with no recall loss at
all, or 61 at $0.934$.

Finally, scope: of 50 curated pipeline rejections only 11 are classifier errors, the rest failing
upstream at the rule layer or at taxon resolution. A classifier contribution addresses a minority
of this pipeline's rejection volume, and we claim no more.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}\itemsep1pt\parskip0pt\topsep2pt
\item An audit of a deployed filter against its own expert-graded output, with the structural
      reason it accepts almost everything (\S\ref{sec:setting}--\S\ref{sec:nofilter}).
\item A mechanism decomposition of its false positives, a bound on component-wise remedies, and a
      measurement of what the reformulation repairs (\S\ref{sec:decomp}, \S\ref{sec:results}).

\item Four negative results and a measurement of why argument order is not currently evaluable
      (\S\ref{sec:notfixed}, \S\ref{sec:negative}).
\end{itemize}

\section{Setting}
\label{sec:setting}

\paragraph{The pipeline under audit.}
BiotXplorer \citep{ruch2024biotxplorer} proposes candidate biotic-interaction triples from the
literature indexed by SIB Literature Services \citep{gobeill2020sibils}. For each passage it
matches taxon surface forms against a taxonomy and interaction surface forms against a vocabulary
derived from the Relation Ontology, and emits every resulting $(s_1, r, s_2)$ combination together
with the passage, the three matched surface strings, their canonical forms, and heuristic
retrieval scores. Candidates are therefore proposed by \emph{co-occurrence}: a passage naming two
taxa near an interaction term produces a candidate whether or not it asserts that interaction
between those two. A filter then decides what reaches a curator, and in the deployed system that
filter is a sentence-level interaction classifier.

\paragraph{Why the classifier's feature is inert at this stage.}
The rule layer runs before the classifier, and it is also what defines the pool. We pulled the
deployment pool in full: $175{,}588$ candidate rows over $165{,}325$ distinct passages and
$115{,}404$ distinct triplet keys. Both taxon surface forms and the interaction surface form are
non-empty on $175{,}588$ of $175{,}588$ rows, because the presence of an interaction term is the
pool's construction filter; an independent lexical scan for GloBI interaction vocabulary fires on
$99.82\%$ of the same passages. Every input the classifier sees at deployment therefore satisfies,
lexically, the property it was trained to discriminate. The generalisation is a partial-input
failure whose cue was installed by the pipeline's own architecture rather than by an annotator: the
missing input is the query the pipeline itself issued.

\paragraph{Benchmarks.}
\textsc{Biotx100} is 100 retrieved candidates graded by a domain expert on four axes: whether each
taxon surface form was correctly resolved ($92/100$ and $98/100$), whether the relation was correct
($90/100$), and whether the whole triple is supported by its passage ($65/100$). It uniquely
records the deployed filter's own accept/reject decision, which is what makes \S\ref{sec:nofilter}
possible. Every row has rank 1, so this is precision at one retrieved candidate.
\textsc{Reject50} is 50 curated pipeline \emph{rejections}, 11 of them true positives wrongly
discarded; it is the only recall-side instrument in this paper, and the deployed filter scores
$0/11$ on it by construction. \textsc{Test287} is a larger pair-level set assembled from expert
curation: 287 items with a recoverable relation term, $0.544$ positive, used here for ranking only.

\paragraph{A query-representation correction.}
Each candidate carries both a canonical string (\texttt{species1\_term}) and the surface form
matched in the passage (\texttt{species1\_form}). These differ on $34$, $38$ and $59$ of 100 rows
for $s_1$, $s_2$ and $r$, and the canonical string is \emph{absent from the passage} on $31$, $27$
and $54$ rows. Scoring a verifier with canonical strings asks it about text it cannot see. An
earlier version of this work did exactly that; every number reported here uses the surface-form
query, the representation the model consumes at inference, and comparisons to our own prior
reports are void.

\paragraph{Evaluation protocol.}
Decision thresholds are fixed before evaluation and never selected on the set being reported, coming
from a held-out development split or a closed-form prior-shift rule. The reported operating point
$\tau = 0.219$ is demonstrably not tuned on \textsc{Biotx100}: the F1-optimal threshold there is
$\approx 0.025$, giving F1 $0.908$ against $0.905$. All comparisons report the accept-everything
baseline, intervals are $20{,}000$-sample bootstraps or Clopper--Pearson, and model figures are
means over three seeds. \textsc{Biotx100} shares no passage with training data; \textsc{Reject50}
shares nine, audited in \S\ref{sec:recall}.

Table~\ref{tab:headline} gives the central observation. The deployed filter accepts 91 of 100
candidates and reaches precision $0.714$ against $0.650$ for accepting everything, a difference
this benchmark cannot resolve ($p = 0.119$; Clopper--Pearson intervals $[0.610, 0.804]$ and
$[0.548, 0.743]$). Its recall is $1.000$: it rejects nothing that should be kept.

Two things must be said precisely here, because the obvious summary --- ``the filter does not
filter'' --- is stronger than the data support. First, the filter is not uninformative. Its accept
set is not independent of whole-triple support: all nine of its rejections are correct, Fisher's
exact test on its $2\times2$ decision table gives $p = 3.7\times10^{-5}$, and against accepting
everything its \emph{accuracy} advantage is significant (exact McNemar on 9 discordant pairs,
$p = 0.004$). Second, what it cannot do is convert that signal into precision, because it accepts
91 of 100 candidates and exposes no way to accept fewer.

The sharpest form of the observation conditions on the sentence-level question already being
answered affirmatively. Among the 90 candidates whose relation the expert graded correct, the
filter accepts 83, and of the 25 that nonetheless fail to support their triple it rejects 7:
precision $0.783$ against a subset base rate of $0.722$, again not separable ($p = 0.131$). The
verifier on the same 90 reaches $0.950$ at 60 kept. Once the sentence-level question is settled,
the filter has little discriminative power left over the question the pipeline actually asks.

The signal it does carry is also not the signal it was trained on. Its decisions reach a Matthews
correlation of $0.128$ with the sentence-level axis it was trained on --- does this passage
describe a biotic interaction --- against $0.560$ with whether both taxa were correctly resolved
and $0.429$ with whole-triple support. A classifier trained to detect interaction language, then
deployed where interaction language is a constant (\S\ref{sec:setting}), ends up acting on
incidental correlates of the question it is not being asked.

The deployed filter's 26 false positives are not homogeneous. We classify each by a fixed cascade
over the expert's three component grades: \emph{entity} if either taxon grade is 0, else
\emph{predicate misbinding} if the relation grade is 0, else \emph{pair-binding}. This yields 7
entity, 8 predicate and 11 pair-binding failures; Table~\ref{tab:examples} gives one worked example
of each. Applied to all 35 unsupported candidates rather than to the 26 the filter accepted, the
same cascade gives 10, 9 and 16.

The pair-binding class is the one that matters architecturally. Its members have both taxa
correctly resolved \emph{and} the correct relation type; the passage simply does not assert that
relation between those two. Typically the relation's true argument is a third organism named in
the same sentence, or the sentence lists several taxa of which only a subset participate.

Two properties make this class hard for anything short of a joint decision: the relation is
genuinely asserted, so relation checking cannot help, and both arguments are genuinely present and
correctly resolved, so entity checking cannot either. What is wrong is a binding that exists only
in the retrieval step's combinatorics. The deployed filter is not blind to the class --- 5 of its 9
correct rejections are pair-binding failures --- it simply rejects too few candidates to matter.

\paragraph{A bound on component-wise verification.}
This bounds any component-wise architecture at every operating point. A verifier that checks
$s_1$, $r$ and $s_2$ separately --- however good each check is --- rejects at most the 19
component-detectable errors among the 35, and has no signal whatever that separates the 16
pair-binding failures from supported triples. Accepting a fraction $r$ of the 65 supported triples
it therefore also accepts all 16, giving precision $65r/(65r+16)$: $0.802$ at full recall and
$0.781$ at $r = 0.877$. The deployed filter sits $0.088$ \emph{below} that bound at full recall, so
it is not merely failing to exploit joint information --- it is losing information the components
alone would supply. Figure~\ref{fig:curve} plots the bound.

\section{Conditioning the decision on the triple}
\label{sec:verifier}

\paragraph{The reformulation.}
Putting the pair in the input is not by itself the mechanism; making the \emph{label move when the
pair moves} is. In earlier work on this pipeline we added the taxon pair to a sentence-level
classifier as a genuine segment pair, changing nothing else, and AUPRC did not move. The reason is
that the question the model was \emph{asked} changed while the question its labels \emph{answered}
did not: with sentence-level targets, two training items sharing a passage but differing in pair
carry the same label, so gradient descent has no reason to attend to the query.

The teacher here is asked, per candidate, whether the passage supports \emph{this} triple, so its
labels vary across pairs on the same passage. It is a locally hosted 32B open-weight instruction
model \citep{qwen2025technical}, prompted zero-shot at temperature 0. On the
\textsc{Biotx100} items for which we hold human gold it reaches precision $0.919$ and F1 $0.898$,
and against 150 human-graded items its overall mislabel rate is $22/150 = 14.7\%$, Clopper--Pearson
$[9.4\%, 21.4\%]$, split 10 false positives and 12 false negatives.

\paragraph{The corpora.}
\textsc{Verifier-base} is distilled from 20{,}000 teacher-labelled rows ($0.289$ positive).
\textsc{Verifier-PC} adds three blocks for a total of 48{,}338 rows ($0.343$ positive): 16{,}546
\emph{pair-contrast} rows, 6{,}201 actively sampled rows, and 5{,}591 rows in which the candidate's
argument order is reversed. The pair-contrast block is the point of the design: it contributes
2{,}017 passages that carry \emph{different labels for different pairs}, of which
\textsc{Verifier-base}'s 19{,}999 distinct passages contain exactly zero. All rows are drawn from
the same rule-layer candidate pool as deployment, so 100\% carry two taxa and an interaction form
and the train/deploy mismatch that produced the original failure is closed by construction.\footnote{%
These correspond to repository tags V1, V2 and V3 respectively; the pair-contrast block was
designed against an earlier, superseded error analysis, so its composition was not targeted at
the decomposition reported in \S\ref{sec:decomp}.}

Pair-contrast rows are built by asking the teacher separately about each candidate a pooled passage
licenses. Where the labels differ, the passage enters as several rows identical in segment~B and
differing only in segment~A and the target --- rows a query-ignoring model cannot fit at all.

\paragraph{Distillation.}
The student is a 110M-parameter cross-encoder reading the candidate triple as segment~A and the
passage as segment~B, roughly $290\times$ smaller than its teacher, trained with a split grouped on
taxon pair so no pair spans training and development. Three seeds throughout.

\subsection{Precision side}
Table~\ref{tab:curve} gives the full operating curve. At $\tau = 0.219$ the verifier reaches
precision $0.934$ and F1 $0.905$, removing 22 of the deployed filter's 26 false positives at a cost
of 8 true positives, and clearing \S\ref{sec:decomp}'s per-component bound by $0.154$ at its own
recall. The precision difference is not a small-sample artefact: Clopper--Pearson intervals
($[0.841, 0.982]$ and $[0.610, 0.804]$) do not overlap, the paired bootstrap interval on the
difference is $[0.137, 0.308]$ over $20{,}000$ resamples, and perturbing gold labels at random at
rates up to $15\%$ leaves the ordering intact in all $20{,}000$ simulations. The F1 difference is
far less secure --- bootstrap interval $[0.001, 0.145]$ --- and we rest no claim on it.

\subsection{The query, not the encoder}
The deployed filter and the verifier differ in input format, architecture, training corpus and
label source at once, so the headline is a deployment comparison and not a controlled ablation. The
one factor we \emph{can} isolate is the query, by removing it at inference with weights and
threshold unchanged (Table~\ref{tab:ablation}). Ablating segment~A collapses ranking from AUPRC
$0.935$ to $0.676$ against a base rate of $0.650$, and F1 from $0.905$ to $0.030$; substituting a
generic relation for the retrieved one, pair held fixed, costs $0.202$ F1, and shuffling the
relation across items costs $0.790$. The decision is carried by the query.

This also settles how to read a comparison between our two corpora.
\textsc{Verifier-base} is query-conditioned as well --- it differs from \textsc{Verifier-PC} in
training data, not in input formulation --- and on \textsc{Biotx100} its global ranking is in fact
better: AUPRC $0.966$ against $0.935$. On \textsc{Test287} the ordering reverses ($0.905$ against
$0.921$, $P(\Delta>0) = 0.98$). Pair-contrast supervision buys operating-point behaviour and
recall-side recovery, not uniformly better ranking, and we report the cell that does not favour us
because omitting it would be selective.

For external reference, the pipeline's own heuristic retrieval scores used as rankers on
\textsc{Biotx100} reach AUPRC $0.842$, $0.808$ and $0.785$, and the 32B teacher reaches precision
$0.919$ and F1 $0.898$ --- which the 110M student matches at $0.934$ and $0.905$.

\label{sec:recall}

\textsc{Reject50} asks the complementary question: of the candidates the pipeline threw away, can
the verifier find the ones it should have kept? The deployed filter scores $0/11$ by construction,
having rejected all 50. At $\tau = 0.219$ the verifier recovers 8 of the 11 while wrongly
re-admitting 5 of the 39 (Fisher's exact $p = 2.8\times10^{-4}$); at $\tau = 0.10$ it recovers
$10/11$ at the same cost of 5.

That headline is inflated and we correct it. Nine of the 50 passages also appear in the verifier's
training data, two of them among the 11 true positives, and both leaked positives are recovered.
Restricted to the 41 unleaked rows, recovery is $6/9$ at $\tau = 0.219$ and $8/9$ at
$\tau = 0.10$, with 2 of 32 correct rejections undone. The interval on $6/9$ is $[0.30, 0.93]$: this
demonstrates that recall-side recovery is possible at the operating threshold, and is not a precise
rate. \textsc{Biotx100} is clean, $0$ of 100.

\subsection{The operating point, against our own interest}
\label{sec:opoint}

At 91 accepts the verifier's confusion matrix is $65/26/0/9$ --- identical to the deployed
filter's. This is arithmetic rather than agreement: with 65 supported triples among 100, any
system accepting 91 candidates incurs at least $91 - 65 = 26$ false positives, and both systems
attain that floor. The two accept sets are nevertheless not the same, agreeing on 90 of 100 items.
We therefore do not beat the deployed filter at its own operating point, and no system could.

What changes is that its operating point is fixed and ours is not. Three consequences are
available to a curator and none were before. At matched \emph{recall} of $1.000$ the verifier
needs 84 reviews at precision $0.774$ rather than 91 at $0.714$: seven fewer reviews, no recall
cost --- though at $n = 100$ this difference is itself not significant ($p = 0.12$). Accepting a
$12\%$ recall loss gives 61 reviews at precision $0.934$, a third less work at a quarter of the
error rate. And a curator who wants everything can still have the 87-candidate point at $0.747$.
The deliverable is a calibrated score with an adjustable threshold, not a better verdict, and a
curator-facing filter should expose the former.

The right threshold is not a property of the model: ingestion into a reference database pays for
false positives and belongs near $\tau = 0.85$, while assembling candidates for expert review pays
for false negatives and belongs near $\tau = 0.001$, where 87 candidates survive at precision
$0.747$ and nothing supported is lost. Those points differ by 33 candidates and $0.20$ precision.
A filter emitting a verdict fixes that choice at training time, for users who were never asked.

\section{Where the reformulation stops}
\label{sec:notfixed}

Four false positives survive the reformulation: one pair-binding, one predicate, two entity
(Figure~\ref{fig:mechanism}). It is tempting to read the per-class repair rates --- $10/11$, $7/8$,
$5/7$ --- as showing which class the reformulation fixes, and we decline: every pairwise comparison
is null (Fisher's exact $p = 1.00$, $0.53$, $0.57$), as cell sizes of 11, 8 and 7 make inevitable.
The decomposition's value is the \emph{partition}, not a differential --- 16 of the 35 unsupported
candidates have every component correct, so no component-wise check reaches them at any operating
point --- and it is that structural fact the reformulation is designed against.

\paragraph{Entity disambiguation, and a gate that cannot fix it.}
The natural remedy for the entity class is to gate candidates on taxonomic validity. We tested it
against the Open Tree Taxonomy \citep{rees2017opentree}. It catches $0$ of the 7 entity failures
and $1$ of the 35 unsupported candidates overall, while discarding $5$ of the $65$ supported ones.
The reason is diagnostic rather than incidental: \emph{Illiger}, Dikarya, \emph{copia} and
\emph{sulla} --- the surnames, clades, transposon families and vernacular names that produced the
entity failures --- all \emph{resolve} in the taxonomy. The failure is disambiguation, of the kind
species-name normalisers are built for \citep{gerner2010linnaeus}, not validity. The gate's
casualties are meanwhile viruses (SARS-CoV-2, tomato spotted wilt virus, orchid fleck virus,
Colorado tick fever virus), which are legitimate interaction partners in this domain.

\paragraph{Argument order: an open problem, and why.}
An ordered query in principle yields direction by argmax over the two orderings. We report no such
number, because the gold standard cannot currently score it. Expert curation marks reversed triples
as correct --- we verified two by hand, (\emph{Breinlia}, host of, \emph{Rattus}) and
(\emph{Demidospermus}, parasitized by, \emph{A.\ inermis}) --- and an LLM adjudicator run over
adjudicable items contradicts plain surface order in 19 of 64 cases ($29.7\%$,
$[18.9\%, 42.4\%]$).

The difficulty is systematic rather than random, and this is the part worth reporting. Among the
5{,}591 teacher-labelled reversals, the rate at which the teacher rejects a reversal is governed
by the relation's \emph{surface string}, not by the relation. Restricted to surface forms with
$n \ge 30$, direction-encoding participles and prepositional forms are rejected at $0.51$--$0.70$
(\emph{affecting} $0.70$, \emph{affected by} $0.61$, \emph{parasite of} $0.57$, \emph{feeds on}
$0.57$), while bare relational nouns are rejected at $0.04$--$0.10$ (\emph{host for} $0.05$,
\emph{infestation} $0.07$, \emph{host of} $0.08$, \emph{pollinators} $0.04$). Whether the
supervision encodes argument order at all depends on which string the rule layer happened to match.
A 193-item human direction annotation is in progress; until it exists, a directional score on this
data would measure the annotation convention rather than the model. \textbf{We make no direction
claim in this paper.}

\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Filters that answer an easier question.}
That a classifier can score well by exploiting how its data were assembled is among the
best-established results in NLP. Partial-input baselines recover much of the label from the
hypothesis alone in natural language inference
\citep{gururangan-etal-2018-annotation,poliak-etal-2018-hypothesis} and from the passage alone in
reading comprehension \citep{kaushik-lipton-2018-much}, and such rules hold in distribution while
failing under deployment \citep{geirhos-etal-2020-shortcut,du-etal-2024-shortcut}; the closest
task-level precedent is fact verification, where a claim-only classifier matches evidence-aware
models because of how the corpus was built \citep{schuster-etal-2019-towards}.
Section~\ref{sec:nofilter} reports that phenomenon rather than a new one. Two things are specific
here: the missing input is the query the pipeline itself issued rather than an annotator artefact
\citep{wiegand-etal-2019-detection,parmar-etal-2023-dont}, and the cue the filter does have is
inert by construction, since the rule layer fires on every candidate in the pool. A cue present on
every input can certify nothing \citep{feng-etal-2019-misleading}.

\paragraph{The argument-ignoring failure is already named.}
Distant supervision assumes that a sentence containing two related entities expresses the relation
\citep{mintz2009distant}, and its standard relaxation exists precisely because it does not
\citep{riedel2010modeling}. Since TACRED an instance has been the triple (sentence, $e_1$, $e_2$)
rather than the sentence \citep{zhang2017position}, and how the pair is injected into the encoder
is worth several F1 points \citep{soares2019matching,wu2019enriching,zhou2022improved}.
\citet{rosenman2020exposing} name our failure exactly, as an ``event heuristic'' that classifies on
(sentence, relation) while ignoring the arguments, and show that QA-style models repair it on a
challenge set. \emph{We claim no part of that diagnosis.} What is ours is that the heuristic is
observable in a deployed system's own expert-graded output rather than in an adversarially
constructed set; that the failures partition into classes with a quantified bound on any
component-wise remedy (\S\ref{sec:decomp}); and that we report the operating-point analysis
honestly (\S\ref{sec:opoint}). The entity-type shortcut usually invoked to explain such errors
\citep{peng2020learning,mtumbuka2024entity} is unavailable here, because both slots are filled from
one inventory of taxon surface forms and type separates nothing.

Attributing failures across pipeline stages is standard, and the accounting is easy to get wrong
\citep{taille-etal-2020-lets,ai2023endtoend}, but it is done across stages on benchmark data; we
attribute errors within a production filter's false positives, against expert grades of the same
items, and report against our own interest that only 11 of 50 curated rejections are ours to fix
(\S\ref{sec:recall}).

\paragraph{Conditioning on the query, and what to hand a curator.}
Casting a relation as a question about a named entity \citep{levy2017zero}, or as an entailment
hypothesis verbalising the relation between two of them \citep{obamuyide2018zero,sainz2021label},
is the reformulation our input format instantiates, and the resulting decision --- does this
passage support this specific claim --- has the shape of scientific claim verification
\citep{wadden2020fact}. Our verifier is a supervised cross-encoder reading the triple as
segment~A \citep{nogueira2019passage}, trained on labels from a prompted teacher, as is established
practice for turning a large model's judgments into a small student
\citep{west2022symbolic,gekhman2023trueteacher}; whether prompting beats supervision at biomedical
extraction is contested
\citep{wadhwa2023revisiting,li2023zeroshotre,jimenezgutierrez2022thinking,brokman2025benchmark},
but that dispute concerns open-schema label assignment and bears here only on the choice of
teacher. The deployment-facing half of the result belongs elsewhere. Selective classification
trades coverage against risk by thresholding a score instead of issuing a verdict
\citep{geifman2017selective}, and that is how automated extraction is made useful to human
abstractors: \citet{swaminathan2024selective} abstain under calibrated thresholds to automate
$57$--$95\%$ of clinical chart abstraction at controlled error. The filter we audit offers no such
control. What the reformulation buys is not a better point but a curve.

\paragraph{Text mining for interaction databases.}
GloBI \citep{poelen2014global} is populated in part by text mining, through semantic-publishing
workflows that route mined interactions into the database \citep{dimitrova2020semantic}; the system
audited here \citep{ruch2024biotxplorer} retrieves over a biodiversity-indexed MEDLINE
\citep{gobeill2020sibils}. Judging whether an extracted fact is correct before it reaches a curator
is an old idea \citep{rodriguezesteban2006imitating}, as is evaluating such tools by whether they
help the curator \citep{hirschman2012biocuration}. The closest precedent for our evaluation design
is \citet{britan2018nexta5}, a deployed curation-support tool scored by the curators of the
database it serves against their own annotations; \citet{lee2018scaling} likewise evaluate a
triage model against a resource's existing practice. Independently of us,
\citet{cuzick2023interspecies} work in a framework that takes the interacting pair, rather than the
passage, as the annotation unit --- the unit our pair-binding class isolates. Large language models
are now applied to interaction extraction at scale
\citep{keck2025extracting,farrell2024landscape,dsouza2025mining,zou2026llm}, but as extractors:
\citet{zou2026llm}, working over citizen-science comments rather than literature, standardise names
and correct categories post hoc without checking extracted interactions back against the text that
produced them. We are not aware of published work that verifies literature-mined
biotic-interaction triples against their source passage before ingestion, which is the gap this
paper occupies.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

A deployed filter accepts 91 of 100 candidates and reaches a precision this benchmark cannot
separate from accepting all of them, because the question it is asked is guaranteed true by the rule
layer above it. Its false positives partition into three mechanisms, 16 of the 35 unsupported
candidates being invisible to any component-wise check at any operating point. Conditioning the
decision on the candidate triple repairs 22 of the 26 and recovers triples the pipeline discarded.
At the deployed operating point the two are arithmetically identical: what changes is that a
curator has a dial where there was a verdict.

\section*{Limitations}

\paragraph{Both benchmarks are small and singly annotated.}
\textsc{Biotx100} has 100 graded candidates and \textsc{Reject50} has 50; every gold label on both
was assigned by one domain expert, no item was graded twice, so we report no inter-annotator
agreement and cannot bound label noise. One item moves precision on \textsc{Biotx100} by one to two
points. The significance of the headline comparison is correspondingly thin: McNemar against the
deployed filter gives $p = 0.018$ with continuity correction and $p = 0.016$ exact, and the result
keeps its sign under every aggregation we tried, but it would not survive a handful of relabelled
items. On the recall side the unleaked denominator is 9 positives, and the interval on the reported
$6/9$ recovery is $[0.30, 0.93]$.

\paragraph{The failure classes are derived, not annotated.}
Mechanism is assigned by a fixed cascade over the expert's three component grades rather than by
direct annotation, so ``pair-binding'' is a residual class and any component-grading error
accumulates in it. That class is also the one that fixes the $0.802$ bound. Cell sizes are 11, 8
and 7, and we claim no differential effectiveness across them (\S\ref{sec:notfixed}).

\paragraph{The deployment comparison is not a controlled ablation.}
The deployed filter and the verifier differ in input formulation, architecture, training corpus and
label source at once. We can show that the query is necessary \emph{within} the verifier
(Table~\ref{tab:ablation}) and that the filter's own feature is inert on the deployment pool, but
we cannot decompose the $26 \rightarrow 4$ reduction across those four factors. The missing cell is
a sentence-only student trained on the same teacher labels, which we did not have the budget to
train.

\paragraph{The training labels are wrong about one time in seven.}
Our training data are labelled by a prompted teacher. Against 150 human-gold items its mislabel
rate is $14.7\%$, $[9.4\%, 21.4\%]$, split 10 false positives and 12 false negatives, and the
student inherits that boundary including its systematic errors; training encoders on
model-generated labels can degrade and destabilise them \citep{lu2025feeding}. The benchmark is
graded by a domain expert rather than by the teacher, so the reported precision is measured after
whatever damage the noise does --- but the verifier's ceiling is the teacher's judgment rather than
a curator's.

\paragraph{Leakage on the recall-side benchmark.}
Nine of \textsc{Reject50}'s 50 passages appear in the verifier's training data, including 2 of its
11 true positives. We report the leakage-free figures in \S\ref{sec:recall} and recommend the
unleaked subset for any future comparison. \textsc{Biotx100} is unaffected ($0/100$).

\paragraph{The classifier owns a minority of the pipeline's failures.}
Of 50 curated rejections, 11 are classifier errors. The rest fail upstream: 32 carry an incorrect
interaction term and 17 a mis-resolved taxon, 14 of them both, and a further 4 are correct
rejections with every component individually right. No classifier change, ours included, addresses
the upstream mass, and our precision gain is not a pipeline-level gain. An earlier internal study
using a different assignment convention reported a $40/34/22$ split across the rule, entity and
classifier layers; the overlap-explicit counts above are what we can reproduce, and only the $22\%$
classifier share is stable across conventions.

\paragraph{Argument order is unevaluable on the current gold standard.}
Expert curation scores reversed triples as correct, so no directional number in this paper would
measure the model rather than the annotation convention (\S\ref{sec:notfixed}).

\paragraph{Prior numbers from this project are void.}
Earlier results were computed with canonical taxon and relation strings that are absent from the
passage on up to 54 of 100 rows. Every number here uses the surface-form query; comparisons to our
own prior reports should not be made.

\paragraph{One system, one domain, one pretrained teacher.}
We audit one retrieval system, one interaction vocabulary and one domain, taxon--taxon interactions
in MEDLINE, so we cannot show that filters over other pools fail the same way, although the
mechanism --- a pair-agnostic decision over passages naming more than one candidate pair --- is not
specific to ours. The passages are published MEDLINE text and the teacher is a pretrained model; we
cannot exclude that it saw them, or the interactions they report, during pretraining, so its
judgments should be read as an optimistic estimate of performance on genuinely new literature.
