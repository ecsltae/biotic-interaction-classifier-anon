## The label column — read this first

The IPMC draft's empirical core is computed against `triples_ok_full` (is the stated
relation the right one, in the stated direction?). The benchmark's semantics, and the
project's, are **species-level**: `triples_ok_species`, do these two taxa interact.

Under the correct column, on the 100-item graded sheet:

| | triples_ok_full | triples_ok_species |
|---|---|---|
| positives | 65 | 76 |
| base rate | 0.650 | 0.760 |
| baseline filter precision (91 accepts) | 0.714 | **0.835** |
| its false positives | 26 | **15** |

**The draft's headline significance does not survive the correction.** At its reported
operating point, McNemar goes from p = 0.018 to **p = 0.584**, and no threshold reaches
p < 0.05 under species labels (best 0.149).

**Two consequences for the argument.**

1. The three-mechanism error taxonomy collapses to two. A wrong *relation term* is not
   a species-level error: 8 of 9 predicate-class rows are species-level positives. The
   baseline's errors go from 11 pair-binding / 8 predicate / 7 entity to
   **8 pair-binding / 0 predicate / 7 entity**. This *strengthens* the thesis —
   pair-binding rises from 42% to 53% of errors, and it is exactly what query
   conditioning addresses. The other half is entity resolution, which the model does
   not fix and the paper should own.
2. The component-wise bound survives with new numbers. Any checker verifying components
   independently is capped at `76r/(76r+13)` — 0.854 at full recall, a *harder* bar than
   the draft's 0.802. The verifier still clears it by **+0.148**. This is the one
   load-bearing claim that survives intact, and it should become the spine of the method
   section.

## Replacement evidence

The claim the draft wanted to make is supported — by a 437-row species-level benchmark
against the baseline's actual recorded decisions, not by a 100-item full-triple audit.

| | precision | recall | F1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| verifier @ 0.50 | 0.852 | 0.959 | **0.902** |
| verifier @ 0.88 | 0.871 | 0.907 | 0.888 |
| baseline filter | 0.850 | 0.785 | 0.816 |

McNemar p = 2.3e-04 at a pre-specified threshold.

**Per block — put this in the main results, not an appendix.** The significance is not
uniform, and a reviewer will find it:

| block | n | prevalence | baseline F1 | verifier F1 | p |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| biotx100 | 97 | 0.77 | 0.915 | 0.919 | 1.00 |
| reject50 | 41 | 0.22 | 0.000 | 0.692 | 1.00 |
| test299 | 299 | 0.54 | 0.787 | 0.911 | 2.8e-05 |
| all | 437 | 0.56 | 0.816 | 0.902 | 2.3e-04 |

biotx100 and reject50 were *defined by the baseline's own accept/reject decisions*, so
it has no false negatives in one and no false positives in the other by construction —
they cannot discriminate. test299 is the only block it was neither tuned nor selected on.

**Frame the claim as prevalence robustness, not raw accuracy.** Across blocks spanning
prevalence 0.31–0.85, one fixed threshold gives an F1 spread of 0.070 for the verifier
against 0.169 for the baseline. Given a *per-block* oracle threshold the baseline
recovers and no single block stays significant — say so, then note that per-block
retuning is unavailable in deployment, and that at a single threshold the baseline's own
gold-fitted ceiling on test299 is F1 0.842 and it still loses at p = 1.2e-03.
